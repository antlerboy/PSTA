Scoring sheet for your Commissioning Compass: system assessment for change
System Assessment using the Eight aspects of commissioning
For your system assessment, you need to score your system on how it is performing on each of the eight aspects of commissioning. First, we will carry out an initial overall score, and then we will do an in depth assessment on each of the eight aspects of commissioning.
Give your system an initial score
Give your place an instinctive score on how you are doing in terms of each of the 'eight aspects'. Do this quickly. It is just your first pass, your gut reaction. 
We will encourage you to revisit this after working through the questions for the aspects. 
Scores are categorised as below. Note that scores of 70 and above have been rare in system-assessments so far. 
0 – 24 Poor (significant risk) 
25 – 49 Fair (lesser/lower risk, improvement required) 
50 – 74 Good (opportunities identified/issues need to be addressed) 
75 – 100 Excellent (no action required)
Now carry out your full system assessment
Next, work through the full system assessment. For each of the eight aspects, you have two scoring sheets below. 
System Assessment questions
Factors that constrain and enable
The system assessment questions: These encourage you to take a deeper look at your system and develop more curiosity. Score your 'system' on each question using the scoring system: 
0 – 24 Poor (significant risk) 
25 – 49 Fair (lesser/lower risk, improvement required) 
50 – 74 Good (opportunities identified/issues need to be addressed) 
75 – 100 Excellent (no action required)
If you are doing this as a team and each person scored individually, do not simply average your results – look at the overall pattern of highs and lows, open up the conversation about what people are seeing in the system to give that score. 
In a workshop, this can be a great way to use appreciative or action enquiry. It will help you to be curious about how stakeholders experience each aspect and what the knock-on impact of that is. This will help too with the next step. 
At the end, we’ll compare your initial gut reactions to your actual score. Again, we ask you to be curious about where there are differences or alignment. What is that telling you? 
Factors that constrain and enable: For each aspect, you are provided with a list of 'factors in place that constrain and enable'. These are important influences on your commissioning, some of which may be blockers (constraints) and others of which may provide opportunities or advantages (enablers). Which hold you/ the system back? Which supports you most to achieve change and outcomes? 
Your key question is: Select the three greatest enablers and the three greatest constraints.
Be curious about the depth of your awareness or understanding of each of the factors. You may wish to explore the factors further before you score them, but this is not essential at this stage. You can pick that up in your action plan later.  
Once you have discussed each of the factors that constrain and enable, select the three most important enablers and the three most important constraints for each of the eight aspects. 
If undertaking this assessment with stakeholders, it is likely that there will be very different perspectives in the system. In your workshops, think about how you can explore these perspectives to give you a deeper understanding of your system relationships. There are not right and wrong answers here. This is to provide you with insight and intelligence to identify where to intervene for the most effective change.

Aspect 1, Whole system design: Work as one place to support and enable wellbeing, sustainability and mutual support.
Aspect 2, Relationships and organisation across the system: Everyone focused on achieving shared goals with aligned incentives and funding  
Aspect 3, Capacity, capability, and confidence: Shape markets, provision, and delivery of all those (place, assets, VCFSE, employees, families, friends, carers, neighbours, community, etc) who enable people can live their lives wel

Aspect 6, Making room to make a difference: Meeting, interpreting, and actively shaping the policy and legal requirements that we have to work within
Aspect 7, Commissioning process: Technically excellent, clear, and robust arrangements
Aspect 8, Models and tactics: Design of delivery and intervention models that are fit for purpose, effective, and innovative, based on real attention to how intervention and infrastructure is structured and delivered
