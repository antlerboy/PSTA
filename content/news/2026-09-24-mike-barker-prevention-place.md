Mike Barker's article, published on 1 September, responds to the Local Government Information Unit's *Future of Local Government: Time to Rebalance*. He asks what happens when councils protect urgent statutory services while the everyday places and relationships that help people stay well lose funding.

The budget problem is familiar: a cut can be recorded as a saving this year, while its consequences may appear later in a different service or organisation. Barker also argues that devolution should preserve councils' ability to shape local places, not only move decisions to a regional centre.

One question for a commissioning or leadership team: which reduction looks affordable within one budget, and who would notice the effects elsewhere? A case for prevention still needs evidence about what works, for whom, and over what timescale.
