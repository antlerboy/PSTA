An academy becomes useful when participants can connect what they are learning with something they can influence. The project needs enough substance to matter and a boundary that makes progress possible during the programme.

Choose a recurring service difficulty, a partnership decision, or an area of avoidable work. Write down who experiences the problem, what is currently known, who can authorise an experiment, and when the group will look again. A project described as ‘transform the council’ gives learners very little purchase. A project examining why a particular referral is repeatedly returned gives them somewhere to begin.

The sponsor’s role is practical. Protect time for inquiry, help the group reach the people it needs to hear, and deal with decisions beyond its authority. Participants should be able to report an unwelcome finding without it becoming a judgement on their performance.

At each review, ask what has been tried, what happened, and what needs to change next. Keep evidence of effects alongside reflections on practice. A project that disproves an attractive assumption can produce valuable learning, provided someone acts on it.
