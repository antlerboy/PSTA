Ruth Bruce, a National Neighbourhood Health Implementation Programme coach at South Yorkshire ICB, describes Doncaster’s use of relational public service in neighbourhood health. The starting point is understanding residents’ lives, including what existing services fail to address.

Caseworkers build relationships with individuals and bring in specialist help where needed. Different neighbourhood prototypes explore different local priorities, while partners across health, social care, housing, public health, and the voluntary sector develop connections around the people they support.

Bruce describes early work, rather than demonstrated population outcomes. Her account identifies practical conditions for learning: time to understand communities, attention to language that partners interpret differently, and senior leaders able to remove obstacles. Prototypes allow the approach to change as residents and practitioners discover what helps.

For commissioners and transformation leaders, the account raises a useful question: can the people doing the work change the arrangements around a resident when those arrangements are getting in the way?
