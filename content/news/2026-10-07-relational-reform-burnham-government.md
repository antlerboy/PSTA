Rich Bell, writing for the Relationships Project, has begun a three-part series on the possibilities and pitfalls of relational reform under the new Burnham government.

The starting point is not simply that relationships matter. It is that public systems built around standardisation, targets, professional categories, and central control can make it hard for people to exercise agency, build connection, or act together. Bell looks at devolution, public-service reform, and social care as places where a different approach may be tested.

There is an important caution too. Moving decisions geographically closer to people does not by itself redistribute power, and relational language can be absorbed by existing machinery without changing how decisions are made.

For commissioners and transformation leaders, the practical question is: which rules, measures, accountabilities, and everyday practices would need to change if relationships were to become part of how the system actually works?

This is commentary on the government's direction, not a statement of settled policy.
