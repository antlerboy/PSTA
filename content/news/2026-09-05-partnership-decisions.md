Partnerships can have a compelling shared ambition and still struggle with a particular decision. Each member answers to a different organisation, budget, or constituency. That is a feature of the work that needs attention.

Take a decision that has returned to several meetings. Ask each participant to describe what they believe is being decided, who will experience the consequences, and what authority they personally hold. The answers often reveal that the group has been discussing several different questions under one heading.

Make the constraints visible. A member may support the purpose while being unable to commit funding, change a service, or share particular information. Identify who can resolve each constraint and whether there is a smaller action the partnership can authorise now.

Agree how dissent and uncertainty will be recorded. Set a review point that allows the group to learn from consequences, including those outside its usual measures. A shared decision record can be brief: purpose, evidence, authority, commitment, unresolved questions, and next review.

Developing systems leadership means practising these conversations on work that matters, then examining what happens when people act.
