Social Investment Business has introduced the Common Ground Institute with a founding essay, ‘A Stake in Common Ground’. It asks whether public institutions are equipped to govern the resources, risks, and responsibilities people now share.

The essay identifies three areas for inquiry: electricity, water, and data; flood, fire, and drought; and the vulnerabilities associated with coming of age, older age, and sickness. It argues that different problems need different forms of governance, with greater capacity for local and regional decisions.

The institute plans to connect charities’ practical experience with contextual data analysis, history, political theory, and policy. Its argument raises a question for public-service leaders: where should authority sit when the effects of decisions cross service and administrative boundaries?

This is a founding argument and proposed direction for work. It offers ideas to examine and debate, rather than evidence that a particular institutional design has already improved outcomes.
