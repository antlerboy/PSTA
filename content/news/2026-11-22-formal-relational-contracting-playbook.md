The **Government Outcomes Lab** and **Public Digital** have put together a [formal-relational contracting playbook](https://contracting.golab.bsg.ox.ac.uk/) for people commissioning and providing complex public services. It moves through shaping the approach, selecting partners, establishing the contract, sustaining the partnership, and learning from what happens.

The starting difficulty is familiar. A service contract has to establish responsibilities, manage public money, and deal fairly with suppliers. Yet a detailed specification written before delivery begins cannot anticipate everything that will happen to the people using the service. Informal flexibility between a commissioner and provider may help, but can leave difficult decisions depending on goodwill or individual relationships.

The playbook asks how learning and adaptation can be built into **procurement, the legal agreement, and contract management**. Its material on sustaining the partnership covers transparency, governance, changing the service, and sharing data. Those aren't substitutes for a formal agreement: they are things the agreement must make possible.

For a real commission, start with four questions. What must stay fixed, including rights and public duties? What can change in response to evidence? Who is authorised to agree a change? How can people using the service challenge whether the arrangement is working?

**The authors say the playbook is unfinished**, with some sections still missing. It is a developing practical resource, not evidence that any particular contract or relationship has succeeded.
