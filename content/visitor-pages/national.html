National Commissioning Academy

A programme for people whose commissioning decisions depend on partners outside their own organisation.

Bring a question from your work. It could be a contract that delivers everything you asked for while the people using the service still struggle to get help. Or several agencies might be paying for support without knowing what the others provide.

January 2027 dates, fees, and applications

What you’ll work on

Start with the people the service exists for. What difference do they want it to make? Compare their experience with what your service measures, what providers are paid to do, and the decisions each partner has the power to take.

You’ll work with people from across public services in facilitated sessions, including shared programme days and smaller action-learning groups. Between sessions, try a change with colleagues or partners and bring back what happened. The group will help you develop a 100-day plan for your own service.

The programme is open to people in local and central government, health, policing, housing, and civil society. You can apply whatever your job title if you have commissioning work you can act on.

Coming with colleagues

A commissioning lead could join with a colleague who delivers the service and a partner who sees what happens to people elsewhere. Ask a senior sponsor to protect team time and help you get decisions made between sessions.

David Mason offers a 20-minute conversation about the work you’d bring and who might take part. Email David about a team booking.

What participants did afterwards

A group from the Office of the Police and Crime Commissioner for Leicestershire developed a 100-day plan for their work with victims and witnesses. Mathew Kendall, then an assistant director at Barnet, described how attending with colleagues helped them act on each session.
